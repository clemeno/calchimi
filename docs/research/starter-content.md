# Starter content: real plants, Processes and Ailments

Research for #6 (map #1), 2026-11-22. Feeds #13 (which content goes into the PoC).

Question: which garden plants, real Processes and common, non-serious Ailments make a credible starter set, with typical symptoms and a list of things to keep out of the game. No dosages.

## Method and source quality

- **Plant uses**: every plant–use claim comes from a current EU herbal monograph by the EMA Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC), read from the EMA page or the monograph PDF. The WHO monographs were not needed: EMA covers all 12 plants and is more recent.
- **Process definitions**: the HMPC patient-leaflet template on herbal teas [S0], which defines infusion, maceration and decoction.
- **Ailment symptoms**: NHS (UK) condition pages.
- **Toxic garden plants**: the Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) harmful-plants list.
- **Evidence level**: nearly every use below is **traditional use**, meaning "exclusively based upon long-standing use" (at least 30 years, 15 of them in the EU). It is not proven by clinical trials. Only peppermint oil (headache; also IBS when taken by mouth) and a valerian dry extract have well-established use.
  - Game text should say "traditionally used for…". That matches the EMA wording and the "not medical advice" note.

## Plants

Each plant gives one or more Resources: the part used, dried unless marked fresh. Every Process listed here uses only water and Garden Resources.

| # | Plant (Latin) | Part used | Process(es) | Remedy (preparation, how used) | Ailments | Cautions (Pharmacy hooks) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Peppermint (*Mentha × piperita*) | Leaf | Drying → infusion | Peppermint tea, drunk | Indigestion (incl. wind) | Not under 4 y; may worsen heartburn/reflux; caution with gallstones | [S1] |

| 2 | Chamomile (*Matricaria recutita*) | Flower heads | Drying → infusion | Chamomile tea (drunk); rinse (mouth); compress (skin); steam inhalation | Indigestion; Common cold; Sore throat & mouth; Sunburn; Minor cuts & grazes | Asteraceae allergy; no full baths with severe heart problems; mostly 12 y+ | [S3] |

| 9 | Valerian (*Valeriana officinalis*) | Root | Drying → cutting → infusion | Valerian tea (drunk); root as bath additive | Mild stress (nervous tension); Trouble sleeping | 12 y+; not in pregnancy; drowsy, so no driving | [S11] |
| 10 | Marshmallow (*Althaea officinalis*) | Root | Drying → cutting → cold maceration | Marshmallow macerate, held in the mouth or drunk | Dry cough with throat irritation; mild stomach discomfort | Throat use 3 y+, stomach use 12 y+ | [S12] |
| 11 | Elder (*Sambucus nigra*) | Flower | Drying → infusion | Elderflower tea, drunk | Common cold (early symptoms) | 12 y+ | [S13] |
| 12 | Rosemary (*Rosmarinus officinalis*) | Leaf | Drying → infusion | Rosemary tea (drunk); rosemary bath | Indigestion; Aching muscles & joints (bath) | 12 y+; not in pregnancy; not on broken skin; not with gallstones or liver disease | [S14] |

Notes:

- **Combinations**: the monographs cover single plants; most say combinations with other plants "are not covered". If the game has blends, give them flavour value, not extra effect.
- **Duration rule**: most monographs say to see a doctor if symptoms worsen or last beyond a set time: usually 1 week for throat, cold, cough and skin uses, and 2 weeks for most digestion, stress and sleep uses (4 weeks for the rosemary bath). This is a ready-made rule for Patients who come back untreated.
- **Dropped**: two monograph uses are left out of the PoC.
  - Fennel for menstrual cramps.
  - Sage for excessive sweating: NHS treats it as a lasting condition (6+ months), which does not fit a one-visit Ailment.

## Ailments

Symptoms are the ones a Patient would report. These are the in-game clues for Medicine (diagnosis).

| Ailment | Typical symptoms | Remedies from the set | Symptom source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indigestion (incl. bloating and wind) | Burning in the chest (heartburn), bloating, feeling full, nausea, belching, wind, bitter fluid coming back up | Peppermint, chamomile, lemon balm, sage, fennel, rosemary teas; marshmallow (mild stomach discomfort) | [N1] |
| Tension headache | Pain on both sides of the head, face or neck; pressure or a tight band around the head; tender to the touch | Peppermint oil, rubbed on (adults) | [N2] |
| Chesty cough (with a cold) | Cough bringing up mucus, alongside cold symptoms; usually clears in 3–4 weeks | Thyme tea, fennel tea | [N3] |
| Dry cough with throat irritation | Dry cough (no mucus) with a dry, scratchy, irritated throat | Marshmallow macerate | [N4], [S12] |
| Sore throat & mouth | Pain when swallowing, dry or scratchy throat, redness at the back of the mouth, slight cough, swollen neck glands | Sage gargle, chamomile rinse, calendula rinse | [N4] |
| Common cold | Blocked or runny nose, sneezing, sore throat, hoarse voice, cough, feeling tired and unwell | Elderflower tea, chamomile steam inhalation, peppermint oil inhalation (12 y+) | [N5] |
| Mild stress | Headaches, muscle aches, upset stomach, trouble focusing, constant worry, feeling swamped, irritability, sleep changes | Lemon balm, lavender, valerian teas; lavender oil bath | [N6] |
| Trouble sleeping | Hard to fall asleep, waking at night or too early, waking unrefreshed, tired and irritable in the day, poor concentration | Valerian, lemon balm, lavender teas; lavender oil bath | [N7] |
| Sunburn / minor skin inflammation | Hot, sore, red or pink skin; later flaking or peeling (blisters mean it is severe) | Calendula compress, chamomile compress, sage compress | [N8] |
| Minor cuts & grazes | Small wound that stops bleeding with basic care and shows no infection; starts healing in a few days | Calendula compress, chamomile compress | [N9] |
| Aching muscles & joints | A localised muscle or joint ache, stiff to move | Rosemary bath, peppermint oil rub | EMA indication wording only [S2], [S14]; no NHS page used |

**Red flags**: the same NHS pages list warning signs that need a doctor, such as:

- a wound with pus or longer than about 5 cm;
- coughing up blood;
- a cough lasting over 3 weeks;
- blistered sunburn.

These could give Medicine a "refer the Patient" outcome instead of a Remedy. That is a design option, not a requirement.

## Processes and Setups

The definitions come from the HMPC herbal-tea leaflet [S0] and the monographs. A Setup name is a suggestion for #13 to settle.

### Core: water and Garden Resources only

| Process | What really happens | Setup | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drying | Herbal substances are "usually dried" plant parts [S0] | Drying rack | All plants (dried leaf, flower, root or fruit) |
| Cutting / crushing (comminution) | Plant parts are cut, crushed or powdered before tea-making [S0] | Mortar and pestle | Fennel fruit (crushed) [S10]; valerian and marshmallow root (cut) |
| Infusion | Pour boiling water on the herb and steep, usually 5–15 min. Suits leaves, flowers and delicate parts [S0] | Kettle and teapot | Teas, gargles, rinses, compresses, baths, steam inhalation |
| Cold maceration | Soak the cut herb in room-temperature water, usually 30 min [S0] | Maceration jar | Marshmallow root [S12] |
| Steam distillation | Steam carries the essential oil out of the fresh plant; the oil is then separated from the water | Still (alembic with condenser) | Peppermint oil [S2], lavender oil [S6] |

These five Processes and five Setups produce a Remedy for every Ailment above. A PoC can start with the drying rack and teapot, and unlock the jar and the still later.

### Later: these need bought supplies

The Glossary says a Resource comes from the Garden or the Mine, so these supplies need a decision.

| Process | Extra input | Setup | Monograph basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tincture (ethanol maceration) | Ethanol | Maceration jar | Tinctures listed for lavender, sage, valerian, calendula [S5], [S8], [S11], [S9] |
| Infused oil and ointment (oil or fat extraction) | Olive oil; hardened vegetable fat or petroleum jelly | Water bath (bain-marie) | Calendula extract in olive oil, or in fat for semi-solid forms [S9] |
| Syrup | Sugar | Stove and pan | Marshmallow "macerate for preparation of syrup" (Austrian Pharmacopoeia 1981, cited in [S12]) |

**Decoction** (simmer in water for 15–30 min, meant for roots and barks [S0]) is a real Process. No plant in this set needs it: valerian tea, for example, is an infusion in its monograph [S11]. Leave it out unless a later plant needs it.

## Keep out of the game

| Item | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Foxglove (*Digitalis*), monkshood (*Aconitum*), deadly nightshade (*Atropa*), lily of the valley (*Convallaria majalis*), yew (*Taxus*), castor oil plant (*Ricinus communis*), thornapple (*Datura*), henbane (*Hyoscyamus*), oleander (*Nerium oleander*) | RHS category B, "toxic if eaten" (monkshood: also avoid skin contact). Do not grow them in the Garden, even as decoration | [K1] |
| Rue (*Ruta*) | "Toxic to skin with sunlight" | [K1] |
| Pennyroyal (*Mentha pulegium*) and its oil | Its pulegone is a liver toxin, with serious and fatal poisonings on record; EMA discourages any use. It is a true mint (same genus as peppermint), so never offer it as a "mint" variant | [K2] |
| Comfrey (*Symphytum*) taken by mouth | Pyrrolizidine alkaloids harm the liver when swallowed. EMA allows skin use only, in adults, for a short time. RHS: "harmful if eaten repeatedly" | [K3], [K1] |
| Arnica (*Arnica montana*) | Skin use only, and "should not be used on broken skin". Too easy to misuse in a game | [K4] |
| St John's wort (*Hypericum perforatum*) | Interacts with other medicines (oral contraceptives, warfarin, ciclosporin, digoxin, theophylline, HIV drugs). The game does not model a Patient's other medicines | [K5] |
| Essential oils for young children, or taken by mouth in-game | Peppermint oil: never under 2 y; breathing problems and airway constriction reported. In the game, keep oils to rubs, baths and inhalation for adult Patients | [S2] |
| Dosages, quantities, strengths | Settled in map #1: real Processes, no real dosages | #1 |

## Open points for #13

- **Bought supplies** (ethanol, oil, fat, sugar): are they shop items or new Resources? Or are tinctures, ointments and syrups simply left out of the PoC? The water-only set above is complete without them.
- **Patient traits**: is age, pregnancy or allergy (Asteraceae, Lamiaceae) a Patient trait? The cautions column turns each into a Pharmacy decision, but each trait adds Patient state.
